# LinkedIn Post — Floops

I looked at a flip-flop and saw a 3,000-year-old bug nobody had filed.

Humanity strapped foam to its feet with a single rubber thong sometime around the Bronze Age, and then just... stopped iterating. No ergonomics. No thought given to the actual mechanics of a hand putting a foot into a shoe. "Traditional" got mistaken for "finished."

So I designed **Floops** — loops for your foot. A molded thumb loop and toe loop built into the strap itself, so the slide goes on with a pull instead of a fight. It's the kind of fix that looks obvious in hindsight, which is usually the sign it's overdue.

But I didn't want to just describe it — I wanted people to *feel* it. So I built the whole thing:

🎨 Product design — 4 colorways, ergonomic hardware modeled and repositioned against actual foot anatomy (medial-lateral vs. toe-heel axes, not just "looks right")
🕹️ A live 3D configurator (Three.js) that extrudes the same geometry as the flat marketing art, so what you see in 3D and in 2D never drifts apart
✋ Drag-to-spin reels + orbit controls — every interaction designed to feel tactile, not just clickable
⚡ Zero build step, zero framework — just fast, deliberate front-end engineering

This project is really a thesis: the most overlooked everyday objects — the ones so ubiquitous nobody questions their form anymore — are exactly where the next real design unlock is hiding. Flip-flops were just the proof of concept.
